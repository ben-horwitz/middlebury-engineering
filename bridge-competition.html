Design · Build · Crush

Bridge Building Competition

By the end of the semester, everyone will be able to design and fabricate a custom wooden truss bridge, then we load them until they break.

Learn engineering by building

The bridge competition is our main program (each fall). Over about eight weekly sessions in the Makerspace, teams of three or four learn the theory and the tools behind real structural design, starting with no experience at all.

Along the way you'll pick up skills every engineer uses: CAD, laser cutting, woodworking, and the basics of stress and strain / truss analysis.

WhenWednesdays, 4:30–6:00 PM, fall semester
WhereMakerspace, Johnson Memorial Center
Teams3–4 people
ExperienceNone needed
What you'll learn

Three skills, one bridge

CAD in Onshape

Design your bridge in Onshape, a free, browser-based CAD tool. Learn sketches, constraints, parts and assemblies, and turn your design into files ready to cut.

Laser cutting

Learn how the laser cutter works, how to run it safely, and how to prepare files in Adobe Illustrator. Your first cut is a keychain; your last is a bridge.

Stress and strain

An intro to solid mechanics: stress and strain, tension and compression, tolerances, and why trusses like the Warren, Pratt and Howe carry load so well.

Where everyone starts

The starter bridge

Every team begins from the same starter design, modeled together in Onshape: a 400 mm long, 150 mm tall Warren-style side truss cut from 6 mm plywood, with slots that lock it to a standard deck and crossmembers.

Along the way you learn to read and make a real engineering drawing, with dimensions and tolerances, just like the one here.

First cut

It starts with a keychain

Before cutting a bridge, everyone designs their own keychain in Adobe Illustrator and cuts and engraves it on the laser. It's a quick win that teaches file setup, cut versus engrave, and safety.

Everyone makes something different, from the MES logo to sports teams!

Want in on the next competition?

The series kicks off at our first meeting each fall. Dates are announced in our GroupMe. Reach out (or just come to a meeting) if you'd like to compete.
